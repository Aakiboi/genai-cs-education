Method

Sample. 30 US institutions, stratified: 22 R1 (10 private, 12 public) and 8 non-R1, across four regions. For each school, one domain-restricted web search found public teaching-center guidance, CS department publications, CS course syllabi and software-engineering courses. These pages were downloaded (HTML/PDF → text).

Coding. A transparent, rule-based codebook (pipeline/code.py, specs/02-coding-analysis.md) codes only sentences that mention AI. It assigns stance (1–5), sentiment (−1…+1), disclosure, collaboration framing, and whether AI is the subject of SE instruction. School-level stance is the median of its course documents.

Validation. All tests are on the validation page.

Limitations

  • Purposive sample: search ranking decided which pages were found, so this is not a census. Each school's few documents may not represent all its courses.
  • Some pages could not be fetched: sites that block automated clients (HTTP 403) and pages rendered only by JavaScript. Failures are listed in data/documents.json.
  • Keyword rules miss irony and nuance, and can pick up unrelated text (e.g., news sidebars). Teaching-center pages list every template option, so they code as "mixed".
  • Sentiment is lexicon-based: integrity language ("violation") reads as negative even when a policy is permissive.
  • Pages are a snapshot from the retrieval date shown in each record; policies change each term.
