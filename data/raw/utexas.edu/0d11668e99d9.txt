Course Objectives:
This is a second course in computer programming. The
purpose of the course is to learn how to use and implement canonical data
structures. The data structures covered include lists, iterators, stacks,
queues, priority queues, trees, binary search trees, balanced binary search
trees, sets, maps, hash tables, heaps, tries, and graphs. The course also
includes basic algorithm analysis/ rough estimate of  number of executable
computations, an introduction to the object oriented concepts of encapsulation,
inheritance, and polymorphism, recursion, canonical sorting and searching
algorithms, testing, debugging, dynamic programming, and functional programming
in Java. Students will be able to implement medium sized
programs using the concepts listed. The course is taught
using Java.

Informal Course Prerequisites:
Mastery of the following basic
programming topics: data types, variables, expressions and operators, control
structures (looping and decision making), procedures( a.k.a. functions, methods,
or subroutines), parameters, arrays (1d and 2d), simple user defined data types
(records, structures, objects), how to apply the previous concepts in the Java
programming language, top down design.
You should be able to design and implement a program in Java based on a
problem statement. The problem should be complex enough that a well designed
solution requires the following:
200 - 300 lines of code
roughly 8 procedures / functions / methods
uses arrays or lists of objects
For example, create from scratch a program that allows two people to play a
game of
connect 4
on the
computer. The display can be a simple text based interface. Both players share
the same keyboard and take turns.

Depending on the results of an exam, exam scores may be adjusted.

. No other class components are
adjusted.

based on your score out of 100 based on the following ranges. Note [
indicates inclusive and ) indicates exclusive. A: >= 92.5, A-: [90.0,

Academic Honesty: TL;DR (too long didn't read) If you
copy code from the web, another student, or use a generative AI, that is cheating in my course. The
teaching staff will catch you.
I shall submit an academic
dishonesty case to the
Office of the Dean of Students
and request penalties up to and including an F in the course.
In the past several years cheating in CS314 has
skyrocketed from 2% to 3% of the students in the course to upwards of 10%. The
increase is due to the availability of generative AI chatbots such as ChatGPT,
Copilot, Perplexity, Claude, etc.
To be clear, this is a foundational course. Generative
and agentic AI are utterly and completely disallowed on all aspects of
programming assignments (solutions, testing, experiments, write-ups, debugging,
etc.), quizzes, and exams. This is a foundational course in programming and per
CS department policy AI use on any course element with points attached
(programming assignments, quizzes, exams) is strictly forbidden.
Chatbots are outstanding tools for learning. We encourage you to use ChatGPT or
generative AIs as part of understanding concepts and materials in the course
(for example when studying class content or studying for exams), but not to do the
work for you. For example, you can ask an LLM to explain what an instance
variable is, why ArrayList.add is amortized constant time, or why the worst case
order of quicksort is O(N^2). You may use generative AIs to help understand why
your program may have failed tests after the submisison deadline has past.

For CS314 the policy on collaboration is modified as follows:
If you are repeating the course you may reuse code you completed on
your own. You may NOT use code from a program you worked on as part of pair
or code that was from a program involved in an academic dishonesty case.
You must start from scratch on any and all programs that:
were part of an academic dishonesty case
you worked with a partner during a pervious semester
you are working with a partner this semester
You are encouraged to study for tests together, to
discuss
at a very high level
methods for solving the assignments, to help each other in using the
software, and to discuss methods for debugging code.
You are committing
academic dishonesty if you look at someone else code (current students,
past students and code from the web) in electronic or printed form OR discuss the code in
at such a detailed level that solutions turn out essentially the same.
You are committing academic dishonesty if you use
a generative AI chatbot such as ChatGPT or agentic AI to generate
your code or fix your code.
You shall not ask anyone to give you a
copy of their code or, conversely, give your code to another student who
asks you for it.
Similarly, you shall not discuss your algorithmic
strategies at such a detailed level that you and your collaborators end up turning
in essentially the same code.
Discuss
very high level approaches together, but do
the coding on your own. Realize with complex problems, two programs that
have produce the same results given the same input will vary significantly
in approach and structure. You are making many, hundreds, of micro
decisions as you design and implement your programs. It is extraordinarily
unlikely two people working on the same complex problem will produce the
same solution.
Likewise, your testing code and experimental code and results must be your own. You may not copy
these from another student.
Examples of cheating
are many and include using a generative or
agentic AI to produce your code for you
or fix your code, copying or referring to
solutions from websites such as CourseHero, GitHub, and PasteBin, accessing another
student's account, looking at someone else's solution code, copying or
downloading someone else's solution code, referring to solutions from
previous semesters, having another student walk you through the solution
and how to code it, discussing the problem at such a detailed that you are
essentially coding together, having another student perform significant debugging of
your code, having another student write your code for you and / or allowing others to copy of
access your solution code. .
This means you
shall not
look on the
internet for code to solve your problems. You shall not use a generative AI
to write code from programming assignments. This list is not all inclusive.
Examples of allowable collaboration
include discussions of general concepts and solution strategies and help with syntax

The code you can reuse in this course are:
Code you wrote in  a previous course, if

Any code you develop with the instructor or TAs in

Code (with attribution) from the class slides and the class coding examples.

Exam Conduct: Requirements for the three out of class exams.
Rationale
:
These requirements may
seem draconian, but as the exams are our primary tool for evaluating your
knowledge and skills in the course content, we must insure the integrity of the
exam. There have been students (granted a very small number of students) in the
past who have cheated on exams in various ways and the requirements below have
come about to combat this cheating. It is disheartening that the actions of a
few require such dramatic measures, but again, we must insure the integrity of
the exams due to their importance in determining knowledge of the course
material and ultimately grades.
If you
are feeling unwell on the day of the exam, do not attend the exam.
Seek medical attention, and obtain appropriate documentation from
UT Student Emergency Services
.
I STRONGLY urge you NOT to bring any personal
electronic devices to the exam. Please leave your phone, tablets, laptops,
smart-watches, and smart-glasses at home.
No outside resources of any kind including
notes, books, or electronic devices (phone, tablet, computer, notes, calculator,
smart glasses, etc.) may be used one exams.
Use of outside resources on an exam is a
violation of the course requirements and shall result in a 0 on the exam. You
may not use outside resources in the exam room or outside the exam room if you
must use the restroom.
Backpacks and bags shall be placed in the front
of the exam room. If you bring any personal electronics they must be placed in
your bag which is then placed at the front of the room. Again, I strongly urge
you NOT to bring ANY personal electronics to the exam.
Having any outside resources  (phone,
tablet, computer, notes, calculator, smart glasses, etc.) on your person during
an exam is also disallowed.
Having outside resources on your person during
an exam is a violation of the course requirements and shall result in a 0 on the
exam. Accessing any electronic resources outside the exam room, during the exam
is also a violation of the course requirements and shall result in a 0 on the
exam.
Bring pencils, pens, erasers, highlighters and your
UTID. You are allowed to use highlighters and do scratch work on the exam. You
may have drinks and cough drops with you.
Other than that I strongly
urge you to leave everything else at home.
Scratch paper shall be provided
Exam Procedures:
Pre Exam:
You must take the exam in your assigned room.
There shall be rosters outside the room with your UTEID and your
exam number.
Note your exam number when you arrive.
Wait to enter the room until the exam proctor present states it is time to
enter.
Upon entering the classroom,
place your bag at the designated area at the front of the classroom.
Bags must remain there for the duration of the exam.
When you enter the room pick up on the order of 4 sheets of scratch paper
from the stacks located at the front and / or back of the room.
Find the exam with your assigned number. The numbers are not seat numbers.
The exam numbers shall be written on the back of the exam in black sharpie.
Leave the exam face down until the exam proctor says it is time to start.
No
outside resources of any kind may be used during the exam. No notes, cards,
books, other people, electronic devices (as detailed below) or other outside
resources may be used during the exam time.
No
electronic devices are permitted during the exam.
This includes cell phones, calculators, laptops, tablets, smart-watches,
headsets, earbuds, electronic smart-glasses, and any other electronic
devices.
All electronic devices must
remain in your bag
at the designated area throughout the exam. You may
not keep any electronic device in your pocket, on your desk, or on your
person.
Communication with other
students is strictly prohibited during the exam.
This includes verbal
communication and communication through chats, email, text messages (SMS),
or any other electronic means. This includes the time in the exam room and
outside the exam room if you require the use of the restroom.
Take everything you need with
you to your seat before the exam begins.
You may
not
return to your bag
during the exam.
Your pockets must be empty.
Do not keep papers, electronic devices, or other unauthorized materials
in your pockets.
Write your exam number in the
provided blanks on every other page.
Place you answers on the exam.
Write darkly and neatly. Failure to do
this may result in the loss of points
as exams are scanned and
graded via computer. We grade what is on the exam turned in.
We do not grade
any answers placed on scratch paper. You may not attach extra sheets to the
exam.
You may do scratch work on the exam
itself as long as your final answer is clearly indicated.
We do not address questions about the content of the exam, during
the exam. (It is a major distraction to those around you and sorry to say,
but some students would use this as an opportunity to fish for more
information and hints on questions, trying to derive an advantage) If you
think a question has an error or ambiguity, state your assumptions clearly
on the exam and answer based on those. Recall, on part 1, the short answer
questions, compile error, runtime error, and infinite loop are possible
answers.
If you arrive after the start of the exam you shall have whatever time is
remaining in that room.
If you finish before the end
of two hours, you may turn in your exam and all scratch paper. If the exam
proctor does not recognize you, they shall ask to see your UTID. Once you
submit your exam, leave the classroom/building promptly.
Do not remain
outside the classroom to discuss the exam or its solutions with other
students.
Restroom breaks are limited to one student at a time.
If you need to use the restroom during the exam, raise your hand and obtain
permission before leaving the classroom.
You may not take your cell phone or any other electronic device with you to
the restroom.
You are also not
permitted to borrow or use another person's cell phone or electronic device
while you are outside the classroom.
Do not leave the classroom
without permission.
If you need to leave for any reason during the exam,
raise your hand and wait for instructions from the instructor or proctor.
