The Opportunity
The invention of photography transformed art, culture, science, and society in ways no one could have imagined.
Nearly two centuries later, with Generative AI, we are at a similar inflection point
—one that demands not revision but new thinking.
Our Vision
Cultivating Innovation for World-Class Education
The AXL cultivates, supports, and shares the faculty, staff, and student innovations needed to keep HMC's promise of world-class undergraduate education in the age of GenAI.
01
(Re)Imagining Higher Education
GenAI tools bring both challenges and opportunities. HMC is well placed to lead discussions of what we teach, why we teach it, and how we teach and assess it.
02
Communities of Practice
Bringing together faculty, students, and staff across campuses to support new work on teaching about, with, and in the presence of GenAI.
03
Inspiring Creativity
Providing access to cutting-edge GenAI tools and supporting collaborative experimentation so HMC stays current in a fast-moving field and leads by example.
04
Showcasing Leadership
Positioning HMC as a leader in envisioning a GenAI future that weighs societal and environmental impacts, raising our profile nationally and internationally.

FA26 Fellows
AXL Fellows
Each semester, a cohort of Harvey Mudd faculty and staff commits to a concrete experiment in a course they are teaching—building new tools, rewriting how learning is assessed, or making GenAI itself the subject. Fellows meet through the semester, report honestly on what worked, and share the results with the college.
Sophia Bahena
Engineering
Replacing take-home exams in Chemical Reactors with scaffolded in-class quizzes every other week, alternating with student-led case studies on reaction systems and their impacts.
Jonathan Chang
Computer Science
Formalizing the lab notebook in Natural Language Processing as a shared living document, shifting grading from generated code toward the visible process behind it.
Lisette de Pillis
Mathematics
Retiring take-home exams in Differential Equations for six short in-class quizzes, each followed by an anonymous live “solution swap” in which students grade a classmate’s work.
Harrison Li
Mathematics
Running a randomized study in Probability and Statistics in which group oral quizzes are AI-enabled or not, testing whether GenAI helps or hinders understanding.
Paul Steinberg
Humanities, Social Sciences, and the Arts
Adding a week on AI governance to Science, Technology and Public Policy, surveying how countries regulate AI and bringing in a guest speaker.
Chris Stone
Computer Science
Devoting four Math+CS Clinic sessions to using coding assistants responsibly—including revisiting AI-generated code weeks later to see who can still explain how it works.
Francis Su
Mathematics
Building interactive, slider-driven visualizations for Real Analysis II—a course whose textbook has no pictures—so students can explore key proofs and form their own conjectures.
TJ Tsai
Engineering
Developing a course chatbot tuned to specific learning objectives that helps students and shows the instructor where understanding is breaking down.
Ten fellows in the inaugural Spring 2026 cohort came before them.
