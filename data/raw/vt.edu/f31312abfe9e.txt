CS 6104: Adv. Topics in Theory of Computation: Further Topics in Quantum Information Theory
CRN: 14012
Instructor: Khatri
Area 1: Algorithms and Theory
Course Description:
This course will cover advanced topics in quantum information theory, including; quantum channels, quantum entropies, multipartite entanglement, coding theory (channel capacity theory), error correction, and learning theory. Students will become up to date on the latest research in these subtopics. A solid foundation in linear algebra, particularly prior coursework in quantum computing (CS 4134 and/or CS 5134 and/or equivalent in physics), is required.
CS 6204:  Adv. Topics in Systems: Cryptographic Privacy-Enhancing Technologies
CRN: 14013
Instructor: Hoang
Area 2: Computer Systems
Course Description:
This course focuses on cryptographic foundations and advanced techniques for building privacy-preserving data systems. Students will learn how modern cryptographic tools enable  computation, search, retrieval, and verification over sensitive data while providing rigorous privacy and security guarantees. Topics may include secure multiparty computation, encrypted search, private information retrieval, oblivious data access, zero-knowledge proofs, verifiable computation, and privacy-preserving machine learning. By the end of the course, students will be able to understand the security goals and threat models behind these technologies, analyze the tradeoffs between privacy, efficiency, and functionality, evaluate representative protocols used in secure data systems, and reason about how these techniques can support emerging applications such as private analytics, secure cloud services, and privacy-preserving retrieval-augmented generation.
CS 6204: Adv. Topics in Systems: Sustainable AI across the Computing Continuum
CRN: 14014
Instructor: Nikolopoulos
Area 2: Computer Systems
Course Description:
This course treats inference for today's large language, vision-language, and diffusion models as a systems problem spanning the whole computing continuum -- from wrist-worn and embedded devices, through edge micro-clouds, to GPU datacenters -- and judges every technique not just by latency and throughput but by its energy, memory, and carbon cost. Topics include speculative and distributed decoding at the edge, asynchronous CPU-GPU execution on memory-constrained GPUs, KV-cache and long-context optimization, quantization- and partitioning-based model serving, diffusion-model serving and precision control, vision-language and multimodal inference efficiency, tiered-memory and GPU-sharing infrastructure, and energe-aware, LLM-guided runtime control. Alongside weekly paper presenations and a reproduction excerise, students work in small teams on a semester-long project that extends an open-source inference system with a new optimization, evaluation, or workload, and report its impact on the accuracy-latency-energy frontier.
CS: 6604: Adv. Topics Data & Info: Data Security and Privacy in the Age of AI
CRN: 14019
Instructor: Kantarcioglu
Area 6: Data and Information
Course Description:
As artificial intelligence becomes embedded in healthcare, finance, cybersecurity, social platforms, and everyday decision-making, protecting the data that powers these systems has become a critical challenge.
Data Security and Privacy in the Age of AI
introduces students to the foundational ideas and modern techniques needed to build AI systems that can use sensitive data responsibility. The course covers access control, secure data processing, confidential computing, anonymization, differential privacy, and other privacy-preserving approaches for AI applications. Students will also explore how AI and machine learning models can be attacked, how private information may be exposed through trained models, and how these risks can be detected and mitigated. By connecting core security and privacy principles with emerging AI threats and defenses, the course equips students with highly relevant skills for research and careers in trustworthy AI, cybersecurity, data science, healthcare analytics, and privacy engineering. It is especially well suited for students who want to understand not only how to build powerful AI systems, but also how to make them secure, privacy-preserving, and worthy of trust.

CS 6804: Adv. Topics in Intelligent Systems: Foundations of Controllable Generative AI: From Pixels to World Models
CRN: 14024
Instructor: Yanardag Delul
Area 9: Intelligent Systems
Course Description
: Generative models can now produce strikingly realistic text, images, audio, and video — but raw generative power is only useful when it can be directed. This seminar examines the foundations of controllable generative AI: the principles and techniques that let us steer what a model produces, how it produces it, and how reliably it follows our intent. Beginning with the core generative paradigms — autoregressive models, diffusion, and flow-based methods — the course builds toward the mechanisms that make generation controllable. We then follow these ideas into one of the field's most active frontiers, controllable video generation and its extension toward interactive world models, where control means not merely shaping a single image but governing the dynamics of a simulated environment over time. Through close reading of recent papers, in-class discussion, and a term project, students will develop both a working command of current methods and a critical perspective on the open problems that define the field. The course will be implemented as a roleplaying seminar on controllable generative AI, in which students rotate through personas — researcher, red-teamer, archaeologist, social impact assessor, and more — to examine the same methods from competing points.

CS 6804: Adv. Topics in Intelligent Systems: World Models
CRN: 83910 (Blacksburg)
Instructor: Thomas
Area 9: Intelligent Systems
Course Description
: World models are AI systems that learn to simulate environments, predicting how the world evolves over time and in response to actions. Long studied in model-based reinforcement learning, world models have become a central focus of machine learning research as video generation models begin to serve as approximate simulators of the physical world and embodied agents learn to play within them. This graduate seminar examines the foundations of world modeling alongside the current research frontier. Topics include latent dynamics models and model-based reinforcement learning; video generation as world simuation; interactive and controllable world generation; world models for robotics and autonomous driving; memory, spatial consistency, and long-horizon prediction; physical reasoning and evaluation; and the robustness, security, and safety of world models. Students will reach and critique current research papers, lead presentations and discussions, and complete a semester-long research project.

CS 6104: Adv. Topics in Theory of Computation: Topics in Quantum Complexity and Cryptography
CRN: 13740 (Blacksburg)
Instructor: Mantri
Area 1: Algorithms and Theory
Course Description:
This graduate seminar explores the complexity of quantum states and transformations and how it shapes quantum algorithms and cryptography. We begin with a short bootcamp on qubits, circuits, and entanglement, then move through core ideas from complexity theory (BQP, QMA, Local Hamiltonian) to advanced topics like Quantum PCP/NLTS, proofs of quantumness, delegation, quantum money, and pseudorandom states and unitaries.  The course is intended for students who want to understand modern computational complexity and cryptography through a quantum lens—especially those from algorithms, theory, security, physics, or data science—with comfort in linear algebra and probability but no prior quantum required. The goal is to explore the results and, more importantly, the questions that bring you to the research frontier. Students will complete a focused research project with a final presentation.

CS 6204: Adv. Topics in Systems: Understanding Disaggregate Memory in Data Centers
CRN: 20956 (Alexandria, In-Person)
CRN: 22351 (Blacksburg ONLY, Online)
Instructor: Noh
Area 2: Computer Systems
Course Description:
Modern datacenter architecture is undergoing a fundamental transformation. Disaggregated memory (DM) decouples memory from compute, treating it as a first-class, network-accessible resource that can be pooled and shared as flexibly as cloud storage. Major IT firms including Meta, Google, Microsoft, and AWS are actively deploying DM systems, while Intel, AMD, and Samsung are investing heavily in their enabling technologies.
In this course, we will explore various technological aspects that make DM possible. Specifically, architectural foundations such as CXL and RDMA that enable near-local remote memory access, performance trade-offs in latency, bandwidth, and consistency that arises through disaggregation, and real-world deployments from industry leaders, among others, will be discussed. To gain hands-on experience, students will be asked to pursue individual projects as part of the course.

CS 6104: Adv. Topics in Theory of Computation: Algorithms for Big Data
CRN: 92276 (Bburg)
Instructor:  Vakilian
Area 1: Algorithms and Theory
Course Description
: This course studies algorithmic techniques for processing massive data sets that exceed the memory capacity of a single machine. These challenges arise in areas such as machine learning, web search, network analysis, signal processing, and scientific computing. We will focus on mathematically rigorous models for designing space-efficient algorithms, along with provable limitations of what can be achieved in these models. Topics include:
Streaming algorithms and sketching for building compact data summaries
Dimensionality reduction methods that preserve geometric structure
Numerical linear algebra for large matrices, including regression, low-rank approximation, and matrix completion
Graph sparsification and sublinear algorithms for large-scale graph data
Impossibility results and lower bounds that characterize the limits of what can be computed efficiently
The course will focus on rigorous models and provable guarantees, with an emphasis on the theoretical foundations of these algorithms, while also drawing connections to practical applications.
Prerequisites:
Mathematical maturity and a solid background in algorithms (e.g., CS5104), discrete probability, and linear algebra.

CRN: 92303 (Online)
Instructor: Noh

Course Description:
In this course, we will concentrate on two key algorithms widely used in contemporary systems. Specifically, cache replacement algorithms and garbage collection algorithms in log structured-based systems (LSS) will be discussed. Caching replacement algorithms have been a topic of research for decades, yet we continually see new developments. Every major IT company, including Google, Meta, and Amazon, is developing its own replacement algorithm at various levels of systems as they are core to enhancing performance. Similarly, the notion of garbage collection algorithms for LSS has been around for decades. Yet, we still see developments of new algorithms as LSS has become fundamental in systems such as SSDs and Key Value Stores (KVS) such as RocksDB. In this course, our goal is to garner a concrete understanding of these algorithms and the developments around these algorithms within systems.
